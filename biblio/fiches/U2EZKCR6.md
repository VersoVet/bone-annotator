# Multistep Automated Data Labelling Procedure (MADLaP) for Thyroid Nodules on Ultrasound: An Artificial Intelligence Approach for Automating Image Annotation

**Auteurs** : Jikai Zhang, Maciej M. Mazurowski, Brian C. Allen, Benjamin Wildman-Torbiner
**Année** : 2022
**DOI** : 10.1016/j.artmed.2023.102553

## Résumé

Machine learning (ML) for diagnosis of thyroid nodules on ultrasound is an active area of research. However, ML tools require large, well-labelled datasets, the curation of which is time-consuming and labor-intensive. The purpose of our study was to develop and test a deep-learning-based tool to facilitate and automate the data annotation process for thyroid nodules; we named our tool Multistep Automated Data Labelling Procedure (MADLaP). MADLaP was designed to take multiple inputs included pathology reports, ultrasound images, and radiology reports. Using multiple step-wise modules including rule-based natural language processing, deep-learning-based imaging segmentation, and optical character recognition, MADLaP automatically identified images of a specific thyroid nodule and correctly assigned a pathology label. The model was developed using a training set of 378 patients across our health system and tested on a separate set of 93 patients. Ground truths for both sets were selected

## Conclusions

MADLaP (Multistep Automated Data Labelling Procedure) can successfully create datasets of US images of thyroid nodules for downstream machine learning tasks by leveraging basic imaging and medical record data (image calipers, embedded text in US images, pathology/radiology text reports) The annotation process was accurate, though its yield was lower than anticipated MADLaP performance varied greatly depending on the location of original image acquisition In full automation, MADLaP could be implemented to curate large datasets using unlabeled, raw data from an institutional database Automating the complex process of radiology image annotation proved challenging, though results suggest that automation is feasible MADLaP may serve as an example for other disease processes in ongoing efforts to reduce the burden of manual labeling
